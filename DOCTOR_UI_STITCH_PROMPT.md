# Stitch prompt: Sahay clinician and account screens

Design the missing **clinician and account experience** for **Sahay**, an India-first emotional wellbeing course prototype for adults affected by cancer. The patient app already exists as a **native Android app in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose**. Use the existing Sahay Stitch visual direction, emblem, warm calm palette, typography, soft cards, and India-first tone. This is the same Android product with role-based navigation, not an iPhone design. Provide phone screens at 360–412 dp and adaptable tablet layouts around 800 dp for a doctor working at a desk. Deliver named screens, component states, and realistic synthetic content that a coding agent can implement in Compose.

**Product boundary:** the AI offers emotional support and prepares visit questions. It does not diagnose, prescribe, alert a clinician, monitor emergencies, or replace medical care. The clinician sees only content a patient deliberately shared. This is a fictional course demo, with no EHR, real hospital branding, or real patient data. Do not depict a live crisis-alert feed, an unstaffed response-time promise, or automated clinical conclusions.

Create these linked flows and screens:

1. **Welcome and role entry:** language choice (English, हिंदी, বাংলা, தமிழ்), clear “I am a patient” and “I am a clinician” choices, AI disclosure, and Help access. A patient can still try private chat without an account; sign-in is needed to share a visit brief. Make the role choice clear without making a patient think a clinician is automatically reading the chat.
2. **Patient account:** sign up, sign in, forgot password, and account settings. Use name or demo ID, mobile number or email, password, show/hide password, validation, loading, incorrect credential, offline, and signed-out states. Show privacy, data controls, and logout. No social login is needed.
3. **Clinician sign in:** clinician accounts are invited by the demo admin. Show invitation acceptance and password setup, sign in, forgot password, session-expired state, and a clear clinician role badge. No self-declared doctor registration.
4. **Patient “Share with my clinician”:** from the existing visit questions screen, let the patient choose an available clinician, preview an editable AI-assisted visit brief, select which questions and notes to share, and confirm explicit consent. The full conversation and voice recording are not included by default. Show success with recipient, time in IST, current status, and “Revoke access.” Add a clear note: “This is for your next visit. For urgent help, contact your care team or 112; this app does not monitor shares.” Show empty, loading, failed, already-shared, and revoked states.
5. **Clinician home / review queue:** a calm, utilitarian screen with counts for New, In review, and Reviewed patient-shared briefs. Each row shows a fictional patient name or demo ID, preferred language, short patient-written reason, share time in IST, and status. Add search/filter and empty/error/loading states. State prominently that this is not an emergency inbox.
6. **Shared brief detail:** show the exact patient-approved content, editable question list snapshot, original Indian-language text and optional AI English translation side by side, source links where a general information card was used, consent timestamp, and an “AI-generated draft — verify with patient” label. The doctor can mark In review, add a private clinician-authored note, and mark Reviewed. Confirm status changes and show who changed them and when. No AI-composed diagnosis, prescription, or sign-off.
7. **Clinician account and history:** profile, role, preferred language, notification preferences for ordinary demo shares only, manual review history, account/session settings, and logout. Use explicit “No automatic emergency notifications.”
8. **Admin bookkeeping:** small admin-only screens to invite/deactivate clinician demo accounts, view patient and clinician account status, see who shared with whom, view consent/revocation and review audit history, and reset synthetic demo data. Never show private unshared conversations to the admin. Include confirmation dialogs for deactivation and reset.

Use synthetic examples with Indian names from varied backgrounds, without inferring religion, language, gender, income, or diagnosis from names. Let the user choose language. Include readable native-script labels and correct mixed-language text examples. Use large tap targets, strong contrast, plain language, clear focus/pressed/disabled states, and layouts that still work at large font size. Keep clinical data visually separate from AI-generated text and from clinician-authored notes. The Help action must be visible on patient screens and must work independently of login.

Please output a connected screen set, reusable components, a concise navigation map, and annotations for consent, role-specific access, loading/error/empty states, and phone-to-tablet adaptation. Avoid backend implementation code and avoid presenting the demo as a clinically monitored service.
